Serialization uses SEAL's file-based save/load through temporary files. Moving those temporary files to RAM (TMPDIR=/dev/shm) changed serialization from 1.27 s to 0.98 s per 5-client round on this Linux machine. Your earlier Windows/OneDrive measurement (12.3 s of 14.1 s) is therefore mostly a file-system artefact, not a CKKS cost.

3 · Bandwidth

Each client's model (241,993 values) is packed into 60 ciphertexts of 4,096 slots. A fresh ciphertext holds two polynomials over three 40–60-bit primes, so the serialized upload is ≈19.9 MB per client per round versus 0.97 MB of float32 weights (≈20.5×). The public context (parameters + public key, ≈465 KB) is sent once per client, not every round.

4 · How to read this

What CKKS protects here: the aggregation server never sees an individual hospital's model, only ciphertexts. It cannot decrypt because the secret key is held by the third-party decryptor, which only receives the sum. What it does not protect: the released global model, a colluding server + decryptor, malicious clients (poisoning), or traffic analysis. Local training is unchanged plaintext PyTorch.

Timings come from one seed on one machine; the plaintext and CKKS twins ran under identical conditions. Byte counts are serialized SEAL payloads (simulated traffic, no network). The global-model download is counted as plaintext in both designs.
